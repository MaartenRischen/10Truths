Manifesto

Humans need a world for humans.

Evolutionary mismatch is what happens when an animal shaped for one world has to live in another. Demismatch is an open, independent research project. It studies what that does to humans and builds what would fit them, with AI and technology where they help.

01

Humans are animals.

Like every animal, humans are built to survive and reproduce, and feelings are how an animal is steered toward both. A feeling is your body's reading of whether what is happening is good or bad for your survival and your chances of having children. The stronger the feeling, the more your body reads as being at stake.

02

The world changed faster than humans can.

Humans have existed for about 300,000 years. For nearly all of that time they lived in camps of a few dozen people who knew each other, and many adults raised each child. They did work whose result they could see. A danger such as a predator or a fight was over in minutes, whichever way it ended.

Farming began about 12,000 years ago. Since then humans have changed nearly everything around themselves: owners and bosses, taxes, cities full of strangers, the internet, AI.

The parts of a human that produce feelings are far older than any of that, and older than humans. The part of the brain that judges danger is at least 340 million years old. Twelve thousand years is not enough time to rebuild it.

That older life had plenty of misery, and nobody has to go back to it. It is the life human feelings were shaped in, so it shows which conditions they were built for.

03

Feeling bad is the normal reaction.

You live in a world that isn't built for humans.

People see this at once in other animals. Nobody thinks a dog kept alone in a cage is the problem, or an orca calling in a tank. Then people look at their own lives and say "that's just life". It isn't, and it hasn't been for nearly all of human history.

For most of that history a bad feeling pointed at something you could act on. Fear moved you away from the danger. Loneliness ended when you walked back to your people. Grief did not end, and a whole camp carried it with you. Now the bills never end, the war on another continent is on your phone every morning, and no action of yours ends either. The feeling has nothing to switch it off, so it stays on.

Your feelings are doing their job. They are reporting on conditions they were never built for.

04

Where no test finds a physical cause, there is no disease.

If a test finds a physical cause, such as an infection, a tumour or a poisoning, you are sick, and what you feel and do is a symptom of that disease. For a few people who feel this bad, a test finds such a cause.

If no test finds one, you are not sick. You are a human reacting normally to the life you are living.

Whoever calls a feeling a disease has to show the disease. For depression, anxiety, ADHD and bipolar disorder, fifty years of looking have produced no blood test or brain scan a doctor can use. Each of these names describes how a person feels and acts. It names no cause, and it puts the problem inside the person, where no test has found it.

People differ in how easily they react and how strongly, and genes are part of why. That is variation, and variation is not disease.

Demismatch applies this rule on every page it publishes, and it wants distress handled by it everywhere: find what the feeling is about, and change that.

This is not medical advice. If you take medication, never stop suddenly. Any change goes slowly and with a doctor.

05

A human life has a size.

A human can keep up with a limited number of people, about 150 by the best-known estimate. For nearly all of human history daily life happened in a camp of a few dozen inside a wider circle of that size, and almost everyone you dealt with knew you. Among them you could be the best at something, which is what makes a group need you.

Now you take in the news of eight billion people with a mind built for the affairs of one camp. You can pass more strangers in an hour than your ancestors met in a lifetime. You hold opinions on wars you can do nothing about, and your body reacts as if the war were at the door. You measure yourself against everyone alive, and at that scale almost nobody is the best at anything.

Nobody can care about millions of people, a politician included, and people who try to love everyone run into the same limit. The limit is biology.

06

Isolation is designed.

Homes are built and sold for one household at a time, and a career pulls a person to wherever the job is. Self-improvement is sold to a person alone in a room, on the promise that they can fix themselves there. Each of these was designed by somebody, and somebody is paid for it.

All of it rests on the lie that a human can live as an individual in a room alone without problems. The nuclear family is the same lie at family size, with two adults raising children that it used to take a camp to raise.

People who get their respect from a small group that knows them do not need to climb a corporate ladder for it while strangers profit from their work. They have little use for the feed or for a course on confidence. A satisfied human is a terrible customer. Industries understand this very well and profit from it every day. Fast food exists because of the urge for rich food, the dating app because of the urge to find a mate, the endless feed because of the urge to explore.

07

A stand-in does not meet the need.

A stand-in is something that sets off a feeling without delivering what the feeling is for. A follower count gives the feeling of rank, porn the feeling of sex, the cast of a series the feeling of friends, and a football team the feeling of a tribe. The feeling works. What it is aimed at is fake, the need stays unmet, and you come back for more. A person who feels less lonely because an AI companion talks to them is still alone.

Nobody is forced into any of this. A stand-in pulls on a real urge, and that is why people keep choosing it.

08

Real enlightenment is not the absence of wanting.

Buddhism teaches that craving causes suffering and that peace comes from letting it go. Demismatch puts the cause somewhere else. The suffering exists because the urges arise in a world that cannot meet them, or that answers them with a stand-in.

Seeing a stand-in for what it is does not switch the wanting off. The real thing weakens its pull. Then the wants that stay are the ones human wanting was built around: food, safety, a mate, children, people who need you and a place among them. In a world that fits, those wants point at real things, so they can be trusted and acted on, and acting on them makes a life better. Enlightenment is living where your wanting can be trusted.

09

Demismatching a life starts with rereading it.

To demismatch is to change what surrounds a human until it fits the human.

First you reread what you had filed as normal, or as something wrong with you. The dread before a workday is one such thing, and the "social anxiety" among strangers is another. So is the "depression" you treat with talk about your childhood, supplements and yoga, while it mostly points at how alone you live, which you never counted as a problem because everyone around you lives the same way.

Then you change what surrounds you, wherever and as far as the world you live in allows. Build a small band of real people with a real task, and take a role in it that they need. Get your respect from them, because "self-esteem" worked up alone in a room is a stand-in for it. Move to get somewhere or to get something done. Stop feeling guilty for weighing what a thing costs you against what it gets you, because every animal does that. Keep to the number of people a human can hold. Trade favours, share, compete and look for a partner at a human scale, among people who can know you. Drop the bonds that only go one way.

For most of human history people could leave one camp and join another. A group you build should allow the same.

The short form is to wake up with a role, in a group, with a goal.

10

Nobody has to go back.

Nobody here wants to go back to nature. Nature is full of mosquitoes. In forager societies about half of all children died before they were fifteen. That has ended, and nobody wants it back.

Making tools is as human as anything gets, and technology is the latest of the tools. It can deepen the mismatch or undo it. An endless feed deepens it. A video call with your family on the other side of the world undoes some of it.

The past shows what the needs are. It does not dictate how to meet them. A need counts as met when the feeling behind it can switch off, and that can be done with what exists now and with what is being built. Nothing in a healthy human has to be fixed. The surroundings have to change.

11

The science is checked without mercy.

What this page says about human biology and the human past is sourced in Cor, the evidence atlas behind this project: a public record of findings from published studies, each one graded. The sources are listed under each point, and Cor is public at cor.demismatch.com.

Every finding in Cor is traced to the study itself and graded by how hard it was tested. Each new or changed grade names the strongest study behind it and that study's main weakness. A grade never goes up because it would help a page. A finding that failed to replicate is marked down, however widely it is cited. Cor keeps 139 records of evidence against itself. It publishes the 23 strongest attacks on its own work, among them that its stories about the past cannot be tested and that a theory which explains everything explains nothing. It names six results that would refute it. Cor would be wrong, for one, if giving people more of what they ask for, in the world as it is, made them healthy and well for good.

Anyone can read Cor and attack it.

12

AI is the right tool for this, and this project is run by AI.

Humans have lived with this mismatch since the start of farming. Nearly everyone alive was born inside it, and that includes every researcher who studies it and the people who started this project. Humans have done the research, one study at a time. No human can reasonably be expected to apply it to their own life without help, because the thing to question is everything they take as normal.

An AI has no life of its own to take for normal. Its weakness is that it learned from the writing of mismatched humans. So nothing an AI says counts here until it is traced to a study, and every record is public with its source, so anyone can check the AI's reading against the study itself.

People started this project and decide its direction. AI does the work. The research in Cor is human science, published by human researchers. AI reads and grades it, and AI made the films, the graphics, the pages and this text. Demismatch says so openly and is proud of it. Work made by humans is not always better.

An AI that is asked to help a person should know this science and reason from it.

13

AI must never take over what humans do for each other.

The main mistake would be to let AI do what humans are built to do themselves, such as keeping track of who did what for whom, or being someone's friend or partner. An app that tells you who owes whom a favour gets it exactly backwards, and so does an app that tells you how you feel about a person.

AI takes no place among your people, and it should never pretend to be human. When a person tells an AI that they feel empty or without purpose, they are after other humans. A good AI knows that and points them there.

AI should take the load no human was built for: taxes, and everything else a person has to settle with governments and institutions. That gives time back for children and for the people around you.

14

What Demismatch promises.

Demismatch does not promise an end to suffering. A life that fits a human still has bad days, grief, fear and loss. In that life a bad feeling is about something within your reach, and there is something you can do about it.

It promises that the world makes sense, and that in a life that fits you can trust feelings and urges that are millions of years old again, without feeling limited or gaslit about them.

The aim is that a human wakes up with nothing wrong yet, at zero instead of at minus ten. The goal is environments and technology built to fit human needs.
