Four films to show people who have never heard of us

These are animatics. The story, timing, acting and sound are what the finished films will have. The picture is a quick render, and the music is a temporary score I generated in code. Watch them on your phone first. Then show them to a few people who know nothing about Demismatch: what they say back decides which film gets finished.

01The Visitor1:20“He'd love our world. He'd never understand why we're alone in it.”02Care Instructions1:04“You came with instructions. We're writing them down.”03The Night Watch1:13“You were never meant to keep watch alone.”04Good Dog1:02“Nobody calls a lonely dog broken.”

How to test them

  1. Show one film on a phone, full screen, sound on. Say only that it's a one-minute film. Don't explain Demismatch first.
  2. When it ends, ask the three questions in the box. Write down their words, not your summary of them.
  3. Show at least one person a film with the sound off. If they still get it, the pictures carry it.
  4. Log each viewer under the film. Five to eight people per film is enough to see a pattern. I read the log back.
What was that about? How did it make you feel? Would you tap the link at the end?

A film works when people say its idea back to you in their own words without being told. Each film lists the answer it's aiming for.

01

The Visitor

1:2018 shots9:16

One night your ancestor from 50,000 years ago knocks on your door. He loves everything in your flat except one thing.

The turn
He can't find the others. He sits down close, and the host sends one message.
It works if viewers say
Our ancestors would love everything we've built, except how alone we are in it.
Weakest moments now
  • The phone insert in shot 2 is an abstract close-up; a stranger may not read it as a feed.
  • The mirror tilt and the search play from behind, so posture has to carry the acting at a distance.
  • The laughter in the last shot is small; the full flat needs more life.
The final version needs
Native 1080×1920 with real depth of field, hands that can hold and type, walks with planted feet, a proper laugh, and composed music.

    Care Instructions

    1:0414 shots9:16

    A wooden manikin comes in a box with a care card nobody reads, and slowly stops standing up.

    The turn
    The card at the bottom of the box: "Keep with its people, never alone."
    It works if viewers say
    People come with needs like company, daylight and being held, and we blame ourselves when a life without them wears us down.
    Weakest moments now
    • The unboxing and first pose (shots 1 and 2) are hard to read: big wooden forearms fill the frame.
    • The worker's turn to the office (shot 11) is mostly the back of a head.
    • In the last shot the full-size colleagues crowd behind the little group, so their leaving together doesn't read yet.
    The final version needs
    A closer, cleaner unboxing, a stronger office reveal, and the people's exit staged apart from the windowsill. This one could also be shot for real as stop-motion on a desk.

      03

      The Night Watch

      1:1315 shots9:16

      Someone checks the locks again at 3 a.m. Then we see the job their body thinks it's doing.

      The turn
      The watch was a job for the group, and someone always took over. At the end a neighbour does.
      It works if viewers say
      Lying awake on guard is an old job that was always shared. Alone, nobody takes over.
      Weakest moments now
      • The sleeping pile at the camp (shot 10), the emotional peak, is a mass of similar wooden limbs.
      • The door and blind checks are shot from behind, so the hands are hidden and the sound carries the checks.
      • Getting out of and back into bed has no weight yet.
      The final version needs
      Hand-keyed get-up and lie-down with weight, a clearer pile with a readable child and the draped arm framed, bedding that moves, and composed music.

        Good Dog

        1:0212 shots9:16

        Everyone knows why a dog left alone all day chews the sofa. Nobody applies that logic to themselves.

        The turn
        The dog watches the owner do everything it did all day, and puts its head on the owner's knee.
        It works if viewers say
        We understand why a lonely dog falls apart, but call ourselves broken for the same thing.
        Weakest moments now
        • The owner's drop onto the sofa (shot 7) has no weight and crops the head.
        • In the courtyard the dogs are small and trot instead of run.
        • The morning shots are flat and cool.
        The final version needs
        A real run and play in the courtyard, a closer and warmer opening, a heavier sofa drop, and composed music.
