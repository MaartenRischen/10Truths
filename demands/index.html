Humans need a world that fits humans

What those in power must change

This page sets out nineteen demands on governments, lawmakers, and the institutions and companies that answer to them. Each one is a change in law, in public money or in how an institution is run. Humans need these changes to live the way they are built to live.

Each demand says what has to change, why, and how humans lived. Under it are the studies it rests on, with what is measured and what is our position.

The standard

01

Write what a human needs into law, and test every law, building plan, school and product against it.

A zoo in the United States is bound by law to a written plan for the psychological well-being of its monkeys and apes, and the plan has to cover the social needs of species that live in groups. For a human, the law asks whether a building will stand and whether a product is safe to swallow. It does not ask whether a street leaves people alone, whether a school fits a child, or whether an app was built to be hard to put down. The list to test against exists. Cor sets out what a human needs and how well each need has been tested.

2 sources: US Code of Federal Regulations, 9 CFR 3.81; Ames 2000; 2 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • US Code of Federal Regulations, 9 CFR 3.81. Anyone who deals in, exhibits or does research on monkeys or apes has to keep and follow a written plan for their psychological well-being. The plan must cover the social needs of species that live in groups.
  • Ames 2000. All 14 polar bears watched at 7 zoos in the British Isles showed repetitive abnormal behaviour. Where enclosures were given natural ground and visual barriers, the bears foraged more and the abnormal behaviour fell. Doctoral thesis, read from the abstract only.

In Cor

  • Cor, the full list of needs

Measured

The zoo rule is law in the United States.

Our position

Human needs belong in law the same way. That no law tests for them today is our reading of the law, and a lawyer has not checked it yet. Cor's list is still growing, and how to measure whether a need is met is one of its open gaps.

02

Count every year how many people have each need met, and judge governments on that number.

Governments are judged on growth, jobs and prices. A country can do well on all three while its people see less of each other every year. In the United States, time spent with friends fell from 60 minutes a day in 2003 to 34 in 2019, and no government was judged on it. Count how many people see someone close to them every day, how many children play with children of other ages, and how many adults could leave their job without losing their home. Publish the numbers every year for every town.

Stop the damage

03

Outlaw the business of stand-ins: feeds, gambling, porn, junk food and AI companions.

A slot machine is put together by teams of up to 300 people, and the industry's own target for it is time on the machine. Food companies tune sugar, fat and salt to the point where craving is highest. In an experiment, American students who use TikTok and Instagram would on average pay to have everyone taken off them, themselves included. Each of these products sets off an urge that was built for something real: winning, food, company, a partner. No person can hold out against a product that hundreds of people were paid to make hard to stop. The law should treat all of them the way it treats tobacco, with no advertising, no sales to children and no design whose target is time spent.

For most of human history a sweet taste came from fruit or honey, and the people you saw every day knew you back.

11 sources: Schüll 2012; Bursztyn et al. 2025; Hall et al. 2019; Moss 2013; 7 more; 3 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Schüll 2012. Slot machines are built from more than 1,200 parts by teams of up to 300 people. They celebrate losses as wins, and the industry designs for time on device. Fieldwork in Las Vegas.
  • Bursztyn et al. 2025. More than 1,000 US college students. Users needed about $55 (TikTok) and $47 (Instagram) to give up their own account for four weeks, and would pay $24 and $6 to have everyone at their university deactivate, themselves included. Students only, mostly women.
  • Hall et al. 2019. 20 adults in a clinic ate each diet for two weeks. On the ultra-processed diet they ate 508 kcal a day more than on the unprocessed one, with the same calories, sugar, salt and fibre put in front of them.
  • Moss 2013. Journalism from industry documents and interviews: food companies tune sugar, fat and salt to a 'bliss point' that maximises craving. It records the practice and does not test its effect.
  • Boyland et al. 2016. Meta-analysis of 18 articles. Children ate more after watching adverts for unhealthy food. Adults did not.
  • Allcott et al. 2025. 35,442 US users were paid to leave Facebook or Instagram for six weeks. Mood improved by 0.06 and 0.04 of a standard deviation, which is small. Working paper, not yet peer reviewed.
  • Allcott, Gentzkow & Song 2022. About 2,000 US adults. Paying people to cut social media use lowered it by 56 minutes a day, and they still used it less six weeks after the payments ended.
  • Hunt et al. 2018. 143 students limited to 10 minutes a day per platform reported less loneliness and depression after three weeks than students who used social media as usual. Read from the abstract only.
  • Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan 2023. A formal model, with no data: a platform that sees only engagement can hold people for long sessions that they get very little from.
  • Wright et al. 2017. Across 50 studies, porn use went with lower sexual and relationship satisfaction in men. Most of the studies are surveys at one point in time, and the link did not hold for women.
  • Muldoon & Parke 2025. An argument, with no data: AI companions set off the bond a person forms with someone who answers, and cannot return it.

In Cor

  • EEA-MATE-08, sexual stimuli tested many times
  • EEA-SOC-15, famous strangers

Measured

How slot machines and processed food are built, what students would pay to be rid of the apps, and what cutting use does to time spent and mood.

Our position

These products belong in one category and should be outlawed as a business. The trials that took a feed away for a few weeks put nothing in its place, so they do not test this demand. Cor has no study with data on AI companions yet and lists that as an open gap.

04

Take psychiatric labels out of law, insurance and schools.

A psychiatric label describes behaviour. No blood test or scan confirms one. In the field trials for the current American manual, two clinicians interviewed the same patients, and for 9 of the 23 diagnoses tested their agreement was questionable or unacceptable. The youngest children in a school year are 38% more likely to be given an ADHD diagnosis than the oldest, so the label follows the birthday. A review of 17 studies found no consistent evidence that low serotonin causes depression, which is what the drug adverts told the public. Law, insurers and schools should stop using a label where no physical defect has been found. Drug advertising to the public should end, and health money should pay for what the person is missing.

Stopping some medications at once is dangerous. Bring those down step by step with a doctor.

8 sources: Regier et al. 2013; Frisira, Holland & Sayal 2025; Layton et al. 2018; Moncrieff et al. 2022; 4 more. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Regier et al. 2013. DSM-5 field trials at 11 centres. Of 23 diagnoses, agreement between two clinicians was very good for 5, good for 9, questionable for 6 and unacceptable for 3. The authors conclude that most diagnoses had good to very good reliability. Read from the abstract only.
  • Frisira, Holland & Sayal 2025. Review of 32 papers from 18 countries. The youngest children in a school year were 38% more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD and 28% more likely to be prescribed ADHD medication.
  • Layton et al. 2018. 407,846 US children. Where the school cutoff is 1 September, children born in August were diagnosed with ADHD 34% more often than children born in September. No such gap in states without that cutoff.
  • Moncrieff et al. 2022. Umbrella review of 17 studies: no consistent evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin. Disputed in print by 35 authors (Jauhar et al. 2023), who hold that the serotonin system is involved in some other way.
  • Chmielewski et al. 2015. 339 patients. Two raters scoring the same recorded interview agreed well (kappa 0.80). A second clinician interviewing the patient a week later agreed much less (0.47).
  • Kotov et al. 2017. A review that reports from the DSM-5 field trials: 40% of the diagnoses tested did not reach even a relaxed cutoff for agreement between two clinicians.
  • Whitely et al. 2019. A review of the late-birthdate effect on ADHD diagnosis and medication in 13 countries.
  • Lacasse & Leo 2005. US adverts for antidepressants largely told the public that the drugs correct a chemical imbalance from a lack of serotonin.

Measured

How often two clinicians agree on a diagnosis, the effect of a child's birthday on the ADHD label, and the missing evidence for low serotonin.

Our position

Only a clear physical defect is a disease. This is point 4 of the manifesto.

05

End compulsory schooling by birth year.

School puts a room full of children born in the same twelve months under one adult who directs them for most of the day. Across forager societies, children grew up in groups of mixed ages that ran their own play. The youngest in a school year get the ADHD label 38% more often, so part of what school calls a disorder is being a few months younger than the child at the next desk. In a review of 74 studies, risky outdoor play went with better physical, mental and social outcomes. The law that makes this one arrangement compulsory should end, and public money should follow any group of mixed ages that stays together for years and learns beside adults at work.

For most of human history children grew up in a group of children of all ages, in and around the camp where the adults lived and worked.

7 sources: Lew-Levy et al. 2017, 2018; Ramsden et al. 2026; Frisira, Holland & Sayal 2025; Dodd, Nesbit & FitzGibbon 2023; 3 more; 1 Cor record. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Lew-Levy et al. 2017, 2018. Two systematic reviews: mixed-age playgroups of children are documented across forager societies.
  • Ramsden et al. 2026. Review of 74 studies of children aged 3 to 12. Risky outdoor play (13 studies) and places that allow it (21 studies) went with better physical, mental, social and cognitive outcomes.
  • Frisira, Holland & Sayal 2025. The youngest children in a school year were 38% more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD. 32 papers, 18 countries.
  • Dodd, Nesbit & FitzGibbon 2023. Children who played adventurously more had slightly fewer anxious and low symptoms and more positive mood.
  • Shaw et al. 2015. Across ten countries, those that gave children more freedom to travel and play without adults ranked higher on child well-being. The authors note that other factors may explain the link.
  • Pellis & Pellis 2009. In rats, play with partners shapes the developing front of the brain. Animal evidence.
  • Lillard et al. 2013. A review found weak and inconsistent evidence that pretend play causes the gains credited to it. The tests it covers are mostly adult-led play training in preschools.

In Cor

  • EEA-CARE-03, mixed-age playgroups tested many times

Measured

Mixed-age playgroups among forager children, tested many times. The birthday effect on ADHD labels, in a meta-analysis of 32 papers. The link between risky outdoor play and better outcomes, in a review of 74 studies.

Our position

Mixing ages shapes how a child develops. That is shown in rats, and we hold it for humans. Compulsory year classes should end.

06

Stop institutions moving people away from their people.

It takes years to know someone well enough to rely on them, and after a move you start again. Among 1.5 million Danes, those who had moved to another town as children were at higher risk in adult life of attempted suicide, violent crime, a psychiatric diagnosis, drug misuse and early death, and the risk was higher still after several moves in one year. After a distant move, American adults got fewer favours and less company from the people they knew. No job posting, benefit rule, housing allocation or study placement should be allowed to require a move away from the people someone relies on.

For most of human history camps split and formed again, and the people you relied on most stayed in your life for decades.

6 sources: Webb, Pedersen & Mok 2016; Oishi & Schimmack 2010; Magdol & Bessel 2003; Kessler et al. 2014; 2 more; 2 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Webb, Pedersen & Mok 2016. 1,475,030 Danes born 1971 to 1997. Moving across a municipal boundary before age 15 went with higher risk of attempted suicide, violent crime, psychiatric diagnosis, substance misuse and early death. Risk was higher for several moves in a year and for moves in early adolescence.
  • Oishi & Schimmack 2010. 7,108 US adults followed for 10 years. More moves in childhood went with lower well-being as an adult. The link held for introverts and not for extraverts. Read from the abstract only.
  • Magdol & Bessel 2003. US adults who moved between two survey waves. Favours and company fell with the distance moved; emotional and financial support did not. Read from the abstract only.
  • Kessler et al. 2014. US families in public housing were offered a move to a richer area by lottery. Boys in those families later had more depression diagnoses (7.1% against 3.5%) and more PTSD. The authors call the analysis exploratory.
  • Wiessner & Huang 2022. Among the Ju/'hoansi, gift partnerships were kept for life across camps up to about 200 km apart. One population.
  • Smith et al. 2018. Hadza camps moved about every 4 to 8 weeks and people changed camps often, while cooperators kept ending up together.

In Cor

  • EEA-SOC-07, how long relationships lasted
  • EEA-SOC-05, camp turnover

Measured

The link between moves in childhood and harm in adult life, in large cohorts that record the move and not the reason for it. What a distant move does to favours and company.

Our position

Institutions should be forbidden to break the ties a person relies on. Foragers changed camp often and kept the same partners for decades, so this demand protects the ties and asks nobody to stay in one place.

07

Keep mother and baby together in every hospital, skin to skin, from birth.

A newborn is built to lie against a body. In many hospitals a baby is still taken away to be weighed, washed and put in a cot. In trials with small newborns, continuous skin-to-skin care cut deaths by 40% compared with the usual care. This should be the rule in every hospital: the baby stays on the mother, or on the father when she can't, and no routine procedure separates them.

For most of human history a baby was carried on the body by day.

3 sources: Conde-Agudelo & Díaz-Rossello 2016; Barry & Paxson 1971; Sellen 2007; 2 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Conde-Agudelo & Díaz-Rossello 2016. Review of trials of kangaroo mother care for low-birthweight babies who were stable: 40% fewer deaths than with conventional care (risk ratio 0.60). The benefit was largest in low- and middle-income countries.
  • Barry & Paxson 1971. In all 173 societies of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample mother and baby slept in the same room, and in at least 76 in the same bed.
  • Sellen 2007. Across non-industrial societies, breastfeeding continued into the second or third year.

In Cor

  • EEA-TOUCH-01, skin contact tested many times
  • EEA-TOUCH-02, sleeping in contact tested many times

Measured

The fall in deaths, in trials with small, stable newborns. Carrying and sleeping in contact across societies, tested many times.

Our position

The same rule should hold for every newborn in every hospital. This demand covers the hospital only.

08

End solitary confinement, and end prisons far from home.

A human who is alone against their will feels in danger. Solitary confinement uses that as the punishment. The United Nations' prison rules, which bind no country, define it as 22 hours or more a day without meaningful human contact and prohibit more than 15 days of it in a row. A prison a day's travel from home cuts every tie the prisoner had left. No prisoner should be held alone, and every prisoner should be held close enough to home for a weekly visit.

3 sources: UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners 2015; Harlow, Dodsworth & Harlow 1965; Snyder-Mackler et al. 2020; 1 Cor record. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners 2015. The Nelson Mandela Rules, adopted by the General Assembly in 2015. Rule 44 defines solitary confinement as 22 hours or more a day without meaningful human contact, and prolonged solitary confinement as more than 15 days in a row. Rule 43 prohibits the prolonged kind. The rules are not binding.
  • Harlow, Dodsworth & Harlow 1965. Infant monkeys kept alone for their first three months came out in what the authors call emotional shock, clutching themselves and rocking. One of six refused to eat and died, and a second had to be force-fed. The rest recovered among monkeys of their age. Animal evidence, 4 to 6 monkeys per group.
  • Snyder-Mackler et al. 2020. Review: how well an animal is tied into its group predicts how long it lives in at least four orders of mammals, humans included.

In Cor

  • M3, social bonding

Measured

What isolation does, in monkeys and across mammals. The UN rules exist and bind no country.

Our position

No prisoner should be held alone or far from home. This follows from what Cor holds on isolation. Cor has no study of prisoners yet, and no record of how forager bands punished beyond mocking, shunning and expelling.

09

Give every dealing with the state a fixed end date.

Fear makes you act, and it stops when the danger is over. When nothing an animal does ends a threat, it stops trying, and that passivity is the default. A court case, a benefit review, an asylum claim or a tax dispute can run for years, and nothing the person waiting can do brings the answer sooner. Every procedure a state runs on a person should have a maximum length in law, and when the date passes, the person should win.

For most of human history a danger was a predator, a fight or a fall, and it was over in minutes.

3 sources: Maier & Seligman 2016; Marmot 2004; Volk & Atkinson 2013; 2 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Maier & Seligman 2016. Fifty years of work on helplessness, mostly in animals: passivity after a long threat is the default. What is learned is that acting works, and that learning switches the passivity off.
  • Marmot 2004. In the Whitehall II study of British civil servants, low control at work went with more heart disease and more depressive symptoms.
  • Volk & Atkinson 2013. About half of forager children died before 15 (48.8% across 20 samples). Hunger and disease were threats no action could end.

In Cor

  • EEA-THREAT-01, threats that end
  • EEA-THREAT-02, threats you can act on

Measured

Passivity as the default answer to a threat that nothing ends, tested many times, mostly in animals. The link between low control and heart disease and depression, in one large cohort.

Our position

Every procedure a state runs on a person should end on a date set in law. That ancestral threats were mostly short and could be acted on is argued in Cor. About half of forager children died before 15, so loss that no one could prevent was common too.

10

Set dark nights and body-clock hours by law.

The body sets its clock by bright days and dark nights. Street lights, lit buildings and screens keep the evening bright, and school and work start by the clock on the wall. Across 43 studies of 231,648 people, living out of step with your own body clock went with higher body weight. Among 60,977 British adults, regular sleep times predicted a longer life better than hours slept. The rules should follow the body: dark streets and bedrooms at night, daylight in every school and workplace, and start times set by when people wake.

For most of human history the only light after dusk was a fire, and people fell asleep about three hours after sunset.

7 sources: Yetish et al. 2015; Arab et al. 2024; Windred et al. 2024; Burns et al. 2021; 3 more; 2 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Yetish et al. 2015. Wrist monitors on Hadza, San and Tsimane adults: sleep began on average 3.3 hours after sunset and lasted about 5.7 to 7.1 hours.
  • Arab et al. 2024. 43 studies, 231,648 people: a larger gap between body clock and social clock went with higher body mass index and 1.2 times the odds of overweight.
  • Windred et al. 2024. 60,977 UK adults: regular sleep and wake times predicted lower mortality more strongly than sleep duration did.
  • Burns et al. 2021. About 400,000 UK adults: each extra hour a day outdoors in daylight went with less low mood and tiredness, fewer insomnia symptoms and lower odds of antidepressant use.
  • Bouman et al. 2023. Review of 68 studies: in the high-quality ones, the gap went with higher body mass index, waist, blood pressure and blood sugar.
  • de la Iglesia et al. 2015. Toba/Qom communities with and without electricity: light exposure and sleep timing measured in both.
  • Samson et al. 2017. 33 Hadza adults over 393 days of recording: 6.25 hours of sleep a night, with stronger daily rhythms than Western populations.

In Cor

  • EEA-CIRC-01, light and dark tested many times
  • EEA-CIRC-02, sleep timing tested many times

Measured

When foragers sleep, and the links between a body clock out of step and weight, blood pressure and mortality.

Our position

The law should set dark nights and start times that follow the body.

Build

11

Rewrite land and building law so people can live in groups.

A human is built to live among a few dozen people it knows. Across 263 forager societies the median camp held 27 people. Building law produces the opposite: one household per plot or per flat, a front door that opens onto strangers, and no ground that a group of households owns together. Nobody fixes that by trying harder to meet the neighbours. Zoning for single households should end. Groups of about fifty people should be able to build and own a street or a block together, with a shared kitchen and shared ground, and towns should be built so that daily life is within walking distance.

For most of human history people lived in a camp of a few dozen adults, each of them known by name.

4 sources: Marlowe 2005; Hill et al. 2011; Hamilton et al. 2007; Singh & Glowacki 2022; 3 Cor records. Full list +Close

Studies and sources

  • Marlowe 2005. 263 forager societies: the median residential group held 27 people of all ages. The range runs from 13 to 250.
  • Hill et al. 2011. 32 forager societies: camps held a mean of 28.2 adults, most of them not close kin.
  • Hamilton et al. 2007. 339 forager societies were organised in nested levels: families of about 4.5, groups of 15.6, residential groups of 54 and periodic gatherings of 165.
  • Singh & Glowacki 2022. Review of 34 regions arguing that larger, settled forager groups also existed in the Late Pleistocene.

  • EEA-SOC-01, layers of 5, 15, 50 and 150 tested many times

Our position

Building law should let about fifty people build and own together.

12

Pay the people who raise children and care for the old.

A baby needs more adults than two. Among foragers, fathers, grandmothers, aunts and older children held and fed a baby alongside its mother. Among 15,696 British infants, a baby whose mother had none of six kinds of help had about eight times the odds of a developmental delay at nine months of a baby whose mother had all six. The state already spends money on childcare and on care for the old, and it pays institutions. That money should go to the grandmother, the aunt, the neighbour and the friend who do the work, and pensions and housing should be built so that old people live among the families they help.

For most of human history a baby was held and fed by its father, grandmothers, aunts and older children as well as its mother, and old people gave food, care and knowledge and were given to in return.

6 sources: Waynforth 2024; Hrdy 2009; Hank & Buber 2009; Meehan, Helfrecht & Quinlan 2014; 2 more; 3 Cor records. Full list +Close

  • Hrdy 2009. Mothers and Others: human babies were raised by fathers, grandmothers, aunts and older children as well as the mother. That shared care is observed by separate field teams among the Hadza, Efe, Aka, !Kung, Agta and Pume. The 'cooperative breeder' reading is Hrdy's interpretation.
  • Hank & Buber 2009. In ten European countries 58% of grandmothers and 49% of grandfathers had looked after a grandchild in the past year.
  • Meehan, Helfrecht & Quinlan 2014. Among 127 Aka children, grandmothers were the helper most linked to better nutrition, clearest from 9 months to 3 years.
  • Strassmann & Garrard 2011. Across 17 populations, surviving maternal grandparents went with lower child mortality. Paternal grandparents did not.
  • Gurven & Kaplan 2007. Living past the reproductive years was common across forager groups.

Our position

Both give the mother more hands. The carers should be the same people for years, and the money should go to them. This rests on Cor's record on carers who stay, which is argued.

13

Make a first child in your twenties affordable.

Forager women had their first child at about 19 or 20. In the United States in 2023 the average was 27.5, and 28.5 in the big cities. The years between being able to have a child and having one grew from about four to about fifteen. People wait because housing, study and pay leave no room for a child before thirty, and they are reading their situation correctly. A government that wants children should make a first child at 22 possible, with a home, an income and study that can be paused and picked up again.

For most of human history a woman had her first child at about 19 or 20, among people who helped her raise it. Women who lived through their fertile years had about six.

  • Gurven & Davison 2019. Forager women who lived through their fertile years had about six births, spaced 3 to 4 years apart.

14

Break large employers into units of at most 150 that run themselves.

A person can keep track of about 150 others. In a larger organisation the people who decide about your work have never met you. Among British civil servants, people with little control over their work had more heart disease and more depressive symptoms than people with more. Every large employer should be split into units of at most 150 that hire their own people, set their own hours and decide how the work is done.

For most of human history people worked beside the few dozen they lived with, and followed whoever was best at the task for as long as they chose to.

15

Fund the missing science: build groups that live matched, and measure them.

Every study in psychology compares people who live mismatched with other people who live mismatched. The healthy controls sleep under electric light, live among strangers and raise children with too few adults, like everyone else. No group lives with every need on this page met while researchers measure it. Public research money should build such groups, a few dozen households each, and measure them for decades.

Studies and sources

  • Kaplan et al. 2017. The Tsimane of Bolivia, who farm, hunt and fish, had the lowest level of coronary artery disease of any population measured.

Money and power

16

Pay everyone a basic income.

A person who can lose their food and home in any month lives under a threat that never ends. No action of theirs makes it go away, and the body's default answer to a threat that nothing ends is to give up. Among British civil servants, low control over one's own life went with heart disease and depression. A basic income paid to every person, with no conditions and no office to satisfy, removes that threat. It also lets a person say no to a boss.

For most of human history food moved between the households of a camp.

17

Cap wealth gaps.

Among foragers the richest and the poorest were close together. On a scale where 0 means everyone has the same and 1 means one person has everything, forager wealth scored 0.25. The United States scored about 0.80 in 2000. In a pooled analysis of 12 studies, people who lived where income gaps were larger had 1.19 times the risk of depression. Large fortunes and inheritances should be taxed down until the gap is back in the range humans lived with.

For most of human history there was no land or herd to own and pass on, and what set people apart was mostly skill and strength.

18

Hand power down to groups of about 150.

Forager bands had no rulers. Decisions were talked through until the group agreed, people followed whoever was best at the task, anyone who tried to dominate was mocked, shunned or thrown out, and a person could leave for another camp. In a state the people in power have never met almost anyone they govern, and nobody can leave. In the American presidential election of 2008, the chance that one vote decided the outcome was about 1 in 60 million for the average voter. Whatever a group of about 150 people can decide should be decided there, with its own budget, by people who know each other and can remove whoever leads. The levels above should keep only what cannot be done smaller.

For most of human history a decision was talked through, face to face, by the people it affected.

19

Have the state's own AI do taxes, permits and forms.

A tax return, a permit, a benefit claim and an insurance form are abstract, they come from no person you can talk to, and a mistake is punished. They take hours that people would otherwise spend with their children and with each other. The state writes these rules, so the state's AI should fill in the state's forms and tell the person the result. AI should take over this work and nothing that humans do for each other.
