Privacy

What happens to what you type

Short version: your messages and the replies are kept for 30 days, with the date and your country, so the replies can be checked. Then they are deleted. Your IP address is kept for two days, only to count your messages, and never next to what you wrote.

Where your words go

Your browser holds the conversation and sends it, with each new message, to the Decode Talk server on demismatch.com, which runs on Cloudflare. The server sends it to OpenRouter, which passes it to Anthropic's model Claude Opus 5.5 to write the reply. OpenRouter's and Anthropic's own terms cover what they keep.

When your words show distress, your last five messages also go, through OpenRouter, to a smaller Anthropic model, Claude Haiku 4.5. It answers with one label: whether to show you the crisis reply with places to get help.

What is stored, for 30 days

Each message you send, the reply it got, the date and time, your country, which message of the conversation it was, whether the crisis reply was shown, and two settings of the tool itself. After 30 days it is deleted automatically.

What is stored without your words

A count of your messages for the day, filed under your IP address and deleted after 48 hours. It enforces the limit of 50 messages a day and holds no text.

For each message, a record with no text and no IP address: how many messages the conversation has had, how long it has run, and the signals and label the safety check worked from. It is kept for 120 days, to tune that check.

What is not stored

No account, because there are none. No name and no email address. No IP address and no city next to your words. Nothing that ties one conversation to another.

Why your words are kept

To check the replies. People bring hard things to Decode Talk, and the only way to know how it answers someone in real trouble is to read what it said.

The text itself cannot be made anonymous. If you describe your job, your street and your family, that describes you. Tell it only what you are fine with having read.

Who reads it

The person who runs Demismatch, on a password-protected page. Nobody else. Once a day an email goes to that person with the number of new messages and how many got the crisis reply. The email holds no text.

Messages that get the crisis reply

Kept and read the same way as the rest.

Having something removed

Email info@demismatch.com and it will be deleted. Say roughly when you used Decode Talk, so it can be found.
