Decode Talk

A private conversation about your own life. You say what is going on, Decode Talk asks a few questions about your ordinary days, and you get one complete decode: what is happening in you, why, the studies behind it, and what to do this week with the people in your life. Then the conversation ends.

Decode Talk works from Cor, the evidence atlas behind everything Demismatch makes. Cor took more than a year to build and holds over 5,000 findings, each checked against its study and graded. An AI asked without Cor answers from its training data, where pop psychology outnumbers research.

One payment, one conversation

How a conversation goes

  1. You write what is going on.
  2. Decode Talk asks three to five short questions, one at a time, about what you treat as normal: who you live with, who you see on an ordinary day, your work, where your family is, what you take, what you did yesterday, and when you last felt the thing you say is missing. The mismatch is usually in the parts of your life you mention as background.
  3. You get the complete decode in one message. It says what this really is and names the systems at work in you, with their age and what they are for. It goes through the details of your own life one by one, says plainly what your own part is, and gives you steps for this week with people you named. Every claim rests on numbered studies, each linked to its record in Cor.
  4. You can ask follow-up questions, up to a set number. Decode Talk tells you how many are left.
  5. The last reply gives the decode in a few lines, the steps for this week and what the feeling will do when it gets its answer. Then the conversation ends.

For adults only.

Built to end

What it never does

Decode Talk keeps no memory between conversations. It has no name and no persona, and it never asks you to come back. It never presents itself as company.

It never says it treats or cures anything. It says what a feeling is for and what the feeling will do when it gets its answer.

Where your account points at a possible physical cause, it says that a doctor's test settles that. Where you take a medication or a drug, the decode says what it stands in for, such as people who need you and give back, and that it gets replaced by that: the dose comes down while the real thing comes in, with a doctor. Where stopping suddenly is dangerous, as with certain drugs and alcohol, it tells you never to stop suddenly. It never gives a dose or a schedule.

Our statement

Decode Talk, therapy and therapy chatbots

What Decode Talk is

Decode Talk is not a therapy chatbot. A therapist is a stranger paid to listen, and a therapy chatbot is software that imitates one. Both stand in for someone who has known you for years, and neither can be that person.

Decode Talk is closer to a librarian. You describe what is going on in your life, it finds what the research on human psychology says about a situation like yours, and it explains that to you with the studies attached. Then it points you to the real people in your life, and the conversation ends.

What therapy does and where we differ

Therapy helps a modest amount. Part of the reason is simple: once a week a real person listens to you, and being heard by a real person makes anyone feel better. Therapists also give people things to work on. But the work is almost always aimed at the person: your thoughts, your self-esteem, how you react. When it is aimed at your life, it rarely goes far enough, and it almost never starts from what a human being is built for. Since the 1980s far more people have been treated for depression, and depression is as common as it was.

Decode Talk works the other way round. It looks first for what in your life does not fit a human being, and for ways to change that, in whatever size of step is possible. It points at the person only where there is a clear defect in the body, and that is for a doctor's test to settle.

Intuition

People say this work needs human intuition. Intuition is the weak point. Your gut feeling about another person is made for people you have known for years. A therapist has to use it on a stranger they see for an hour a week, and all they know is what that stranger tells them. The research backs this up. In studies that compare a clinician's judgment with a simple formula, the formula does better, and that is true for experienced clinicians too. And therapists do not get better results as they gain experience.

Objectivity

No reading of a person is fully objective. But one person's intuition and the scientific method are far apart. An AI on its own repeats what it picked up online, where pop psychology and bro science outnumber research. Decode Talk works from Cor, a collection of peer-reviewed studies graded by how strong the evidence is. Every claim it makes comes with the study behind it, so you can check it yourself.

Danger

If a message reads as someone being at risk of harming themselves or someone else, Decode Talk stops, gives the helpline for their country, and does not charge for the conversation. Someone in danger needs a real person right away.

Ending

"You should see someone" has become the standard answer to a person who is struggling. It sends them to a stranger, and away from the people who know them. We say the opposite. Start with your own people. If you have too few, that is the first thing to fix.

I feel like I don't have the same range of emotions anymore as I had when I was a kid. Everything just kind of feels flat, with maybe a few peaks here and there of elation or sadness. I cared for my goldfish and mostly spent time on the computer. Then I went to work. And just kind of waited until that was done, and then went home and watched Netflix.

Person

Person

Decode Talk

What this really is

You are a group animal living without a group. Two old systems in you have reacted the way they react in any mammal that gets cut off. One is sounding an alarm and the other has turned itself down. The panicky sadness is the alarm. The flat feeling is the other one.

Nothing you describe is a disease. It is what a healthy human does in the days you have.

The two systems

The first ties you to your people and hurts when you are cut off from them. It is as old as mammals, 165 to 200 million years. A mammal separated from its group first protests with agitation and panic. If nobody comes, it goes quiet and stops trying [1]. Being cut off was a danger to your life, and it still is: people with weak ties die sooner [2]. That is why your sadness has panic in it.

The second is the drive that makes an animal curious and eager about what is around it. Its reward pathway is at least 165 million years old. Being amazed at things is this drive running. In the quiet stage after the panic, it turns down [1].

You did not lose your amazement by growing up. Childhood is the last time most people spend every day among people who know them. For nearly all of human history that never stopped, because a person lived in a camp of a few dozen.

How it got here

Take yesterday, piece by piece.

The soccer match. The one conversation you had in person was about a game neither of you played in. Following a team is a stand-in for belonging to a group. It gives you a side and an enemy, and the body takes it for real: fans' testosterone rises when their team wins and falls when it loses [3]. A group you belong to knows your name, needs you and shows up when you are in trouble. Your team does not know you exist. The man you talked to does, and he is the real part of that conversation.

WhatsApp with your mother. She is real and she wants to see you. Friends bond most in person and least by text [4]. The one tie to family in your day went through the weakest way there is to be in contact.

Netflix. In the evening you watch faces that feel familiar and have never seen yours. For nearly all of human history, a person you saw and heard that often knew you back. An evening of it feels a bit like company, and you are still alone at the end of it.

The job. You work for a company that puts up buildings anyone can point at, and your part is typing in the figures. The people on site see at the end of the day what they did. A third system tracks whether what you do changes anything. Its default is to go passive, and it only switches on when your actions visibly work. That was found in rats, in a brain circuit humans share [5]. So you wait for the day to end.

The goldfish. You looked after the one living thing that needed you yesterday, and it was a goldfish. The urge to look after someone works fine in you.

"Attacks I might have." Those are your words. You take the propranolol for attacks that have not come yet, so the alarm is turned down before it goes off.

The wedding. The one day it all came back, your whole family was in the room. You were drunk as well, but you are stoned every night and it does not come back then.

Your part

You manage the alarm and leave it unanswered. The propranolol takes the pounding heart out of the attacks, the joints get you through the evening, and Netflix fills it. All three make it easier to leave your days as they are. Your mother wants to see you and you have not set a date.

What you take

If no doctor has tested your heart and thyroid for those attacks, get that done. A test settles whether there is a physical cause.

Where there is none, the attacks are the alarm. The propranolol and the joints stand in for what the alarm is asking for, which is people who need you and give back. They get replaced by that. The dose comes down while the real thing comes in, with a doctor. Never stop propranolol suddenly: stopping a beta blocker at once can be dangerous for the heart.

So now what

The full version is days spent among a small number of people who know you, where some of them need you and you need them. This week:

  1. Set the date with your mother today, and go to her if that is where the rest of your family is.
  2. Ask your brother what he needs help with this month, and do it in person.
  3. Watch the next match with the man from work, in the same room. The match is still a stand-in. The evening with him is real, and that is what you are there for, so nothing is lost if the team loses. After that, find one thing he needs a hand with, because two men who only ever talk soccer stay strangers.

When the alarm gets its answer it stops, and the interest in things comes back after it.
